What counts as an exoplanet

Every TASA verdict comes from the same three tests. An object has to pass all of them.

Orbits another star

An exoplanet circles a star that is not the Sun. Mars, Jupiter and Pluto fail this test, however far away they look.

Stays under 13 Jupiter masses

Heavier objects can fuse deuterium and count as brown dwarfs. Below that line, an object can be a planet.

Has a validated detection

A candidate becomes confirmed once its signal is checked and published. Until then it is a candidate, not a planet.

How astronomers find them
TransitStarlight dips as a planet crosses the star.
Radial velocityThe star wobbles toward and away from us.
MicrolensingA planet's gravity bends light from a star behind it.
Direct imagingA photograph of the planet itself, mostly young giants.
AstrometryA tiny sideways shift in the star's position.
