How Machine Learning Detects Exoplanets

Trained on 7,316 Kepler Objects of Interest (KOIs), our model learned to distinguish genuine exoplanets from astrophysical impostors and noise.

Transit Photometry

When a planet crosses in front of its star, it blocks a tiny fraction of starlight. The duration and depth reveal the planet's radius and orbital velocity.

Eclipsing Binary Rejection

Two orbiting stars produce massive dips (>20,000 ppm) and calculated radii exceeding 25–40 Earth radii. The model immediately flags these as false positives.

Signal-to-Noise Filtering

Permutation importance proved that Model SNR is the #1 decision driver. Weak signals (<10 SNR) are often detector jitter or stellar flares, not planets.
